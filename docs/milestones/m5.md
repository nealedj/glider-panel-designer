## What was delivered

### Python discovery tools (`tools/`, maintainers only)

```sh
python -m discover status | check-adapters | run [--live] | import-reviewed <file>
```

- **Polite fetcher:**
  - obeys robots.txt (and any longer Crawl-delay);
  - one request a second per host at most;
  - identifies itself with your contact address;
  - caches everything, so a re-run is free and an interrupted run resumes;
  - refuses live requests without `--live` and a contact address, and runs from the cache by default.
- **Generic retailer adapter:** walks category pages to product pages on the shop's own site, skipping the basket, login, search and anything robots.txt forbids. From each page it reads the name, maker, price, image, spec text and manual links, using the shop's standard metadata (Open Graph, schema.org). The shop's address and rules live only in `local/discovery.toml`, which is git-ignored; the committed `discovery.example.toml` uses a placeholder.
- **Maker adapters** for LXNAV, FLARM, Trig, f.u.n.k.e., Air Avionics, Naviter, Winter, PilotAware, XCVario and TQ (KRT2). Adding a maker is one small file.
  - The LXNAV manuals table was read live and parsed accordingly.
  - The XCVario manuals, the Air Avionics display manual and the TQ KRT2 manual are seeded from addresses I found.
  - The other six start from addresses I couldn't confirm; `check-adapters --live` will show what they find.
  - Ranking keeps English only, prefers installation manuals, and understands range names ("S8x, S10x" covers the S80 and S100).
- **Spec parsing:** rules for cut-outs (round and rectangular), W × H × D, depth behind the panel, protrusion, weight (g or kg), current (mA, A, or W at V), supply range and connector names. Every value keeps its verbatim line, source, page, method, confidence and a note.
  - **The S100 trap:** a line that names another model gets under 30 % confidence and the note "This line names S10, not S100". That includes models no adapter knows, such as the S8D. A line listing several models ("Depth: S100 98 mm, S80 101 mm") is split per model.
- **Manual parsing:** finds the dimension and pinout pages, extracts candidates with page numbers, reads ruled pinout tables (pin, function, signal type, direction, connector name), and renders those pages as images for the reviewer.
- **Face images** from product photos (`--faces`): background removed, cropped, round or rectangular, scaled from the bezel size. They stay local only.
- **Importer:** checks each reviewed part and writes it to `catalogue/parts/`. It refuses any value sourced from the retailer's site, verified fields without snippets, and generic parts.

### The Review tab

- Open a candidates file. The product list shows search, filters (All, Reference panel, To do, Done) and per-product progress, with the overall and reference-panel counts at the top.
- Each field (cut-out, bezel, screw holes, protrusion, depth, body, weight, current, voltage, pinouts, datasheet) shows every candidate with:
  - its value;
  - its confidence;
  - where it came from (maker manual and page, or the shop page, which stays local);
  - its snippet with the figures highlighted;
  - its note.

  Accept, Reject and Undo act on each candidate. Pinouts can accept several connectors.
- When no candidate is right, or a figure is only on a drawing, enter the value from a named document and page, with what you read it from. The manual pages are shown alongside (click to enlarge).
- The right panel previews the catalogue entry: a front-face sketch, the facts, and what is left out of the export and why. Values that only the shop gave can be accepted for your own reference, but never go into the catalogue.
- Decisions are saved in the browser straight away and survive a reload.
- **Export reviewed parts** writes the bundle for `import-reviewed`.
