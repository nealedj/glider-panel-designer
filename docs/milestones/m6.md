## What was delivered

### Wire sizing from published charts

`app/src/wiring/charts.ts` holds the tables from **FAA AC 43.13-1B CHG 1**, chapter 11, read from the FAA's own PDF. Each table carries its source and its own wording:

- **Table 11-9:** copper wire current in bundles at 105, 150 and 200 °C, and resistance per length.
- **Table 11-3:** the largest fuse or breaker per wire size.
- **Table 11-6:** allowable voltage drop.

Sizes show in mm² with the AWG alongside. A metric size such as 0.5 mm² is rated as the next smaller chart size (AWG 22), so it is never rated above the chart.

- **Automatic sizing:** each wire gets the smallest chart size that carries its current, keeps its share of the drop limit, and is protected by the fuse or breaker upstream ("protect the wire, not the equipment"). You can fix a size by hand instead.
- **Settings:** the drop limit, wire temperature rating, smallest size, fuse load limit and end allowance are all project settings.

### Circuit analysis and checks

Power paths are traced from the battery through switches and fuses to every powered part, and back from its ground pin.

| Check | Severity |
| --- | --- |
| Power path from the battery with no fuse or breaker | Error |
| Wire too small for its fuse or breaker (table 11-3) | Error |
| Wire current over its rating | Error |
| Power or ground pin left unconnected | Error |
| Short (power joined to ground) | Error |
| Incompatible pins joined (power with data or audio) | Error |
| TX joined to TX | Error |
| Mixed pitot, static or TE lines, or a tube on a pin | Error |
| Voltage drop over the limit | Warning |
| Fuse load over 80 % of its rating | Warning |
| Wire from the battery with no protection | Warning |
| Current draw unknown | Warning |
| Instrument port left open | Warning |
| Bundle through a part at the same depth | Warning |
| Inputs joined with no output | Warning |
| Estimated endurance from battery capacity and total draw | Info |
| Lengths that are straight-line estimates | Info |

### Lengths and bundles

- **Length:** route length, plus the depth changes in 3D, plus the off-panel run for parts beyond the panel, plus an end allowance per termination. A wire with no route is a straight-line estimate, flagged as such.
- **Bundles:** each route's bundle diameter is estimated from the wires and tubes it carries, drawn at that width in the rear view, and checked against part bodies.

### Editor

- **Wire tool (W):** in the rear view or the schematic, click a pin or port, then the one it connects to.
  - Nets are worked out from the wires, keeping your names.
  - New wires get a colour, size and label by net type (W001, P01, S01, T01).
  - In the rear view, a connector's pins are fanned out so each can be clicked.
- **Route tool (L):** in the rear view, click points at the set depth, then double-click or press Enter.
  - Wires selected at the time go onto the new route.
  - **Put selected wires on selected route** is in the Arrange menu, and a wire's inspector also has a route choice.
- **Off-panel parts** (battery, inline main fuse, speaker, antennas, stick PTT, pitot and static sources, TE probe) show in the rear view as exit points, with their run beyond the panel. Parts sharing an exit stack their labels.
- **Pneumatics:**
  - tubes join ports;
  - tees are small parts whose three ports are one line;
  - tubes are drawn in their colours and can be hidden in the schematic.
- **Schematic tab:**
  - laid out automatically;
  - drag blocks to tidy (positions are saved apart from physical ones); **Reset schematic layout** clears them;
  - filters for power, ground, data, audio, RF, switch and pneumatic;
  - symbols for battery, switch, fuse, breaker, ground, antenna, speaker, tee and pressure source.
- **Inspectors:**
  - **Wire:** label, net name, colour, size (automatic or fixed, with the reason), length (route or estimate), current, rating, drop, protection and route.
  - **Route:** name, depth, length, bundle and the wires it carries.
  - **Off-panel part:** its run beyond the panel.
  - **Project:** a Wiring section with the settings.
- **New generic parts** (all unverified placeholders): ASI and altimeter with ports, mechanical and electric varios, radio, flight computer, FLARM unit, tee, battery, inline fuse, speaker, antenna, pitot, static, TE probe and stick PTT.

## Choices made without asking (rule 11)

These touch safety, so they are all in `docs/questions.md` (questions 17–20) for you to confirm:

- **Wire rating:** the conservative 105 °C column of table 11-9.
- **Drop limit:** 0.5 V, the AC's figure for a 14 V system.
- **Fuse load limit:** 80 % of a fuse or breaker's rating.
- **Smallest wire:** AWG 22.
- **End allowance:** 50 mm per wire end.
- **Colours and tube bore:** my choices, listed in question 18.
- **Generic placeholder currents and battery capacity:** placeholders until the catalogue has real figures.

A battery-to-main-fuse link beyond the panel is the one unprotected run the checks accept without a warning.

## Not in M6

- **Auto-route:** optional in the brief; parked.
- **Manufacturer cables** with their own conductor colours: parked.
- **Multi-pole switches and relays:** switches are two-pin and taken as closed.
- **AC 43.13-1B paragraph 11-69 corrections**, which would give less conservative ratings: parked.
- **Checking lengths against a real loom:** in M7.
