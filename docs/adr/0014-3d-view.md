# 0014. A read-only 3D view with three.js

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-28

## Context

The brief keeps 3D modelling out of scope: the rear view is 2.5D (ADR 0007), and clashes are
found as plan overlap plus depth-band overlap. A flat rear view makes stacking hard to judge,
though: which body stands out furthest, how a unit mounted on another sits, and where the looms
run in depth. The project already holds everything a 3D picture needs. That is the panel
outline and thickness, cutouts and bezels with their protrusion, each body's outline and depth
band, and routes and wire paths in 3D (ADR 0012).

David asked for 3D rendering on 2026-10-28 and chose a **read-only view**, not 3D editing or
3D export. 3D modelling stays out of scope.

## Decision

- **A fourth editor view, "3D"**, beside Front, Rear and Schematic.
  - It only looks. Clicking selects a part, wire or loom, in step with the inspector, the
    objects tree and the other views.
  - Every edit is still made through the inspector or the 2D views, as ops through
    `DocStore.dispatch`. Undo and delete work from the 3D view.
- **What is drawn**, built by `view3d/model3d.ts` with no renderer attached, so it is unit-tested:
  - The **panel plate**, the template outline extruded through `[-thickness, 0]`. Every cut is
    a hole in it: part cutouts, screw holes and the template's fixings.
  - **Bezels**, standing proud of the pilot's face by `front.protrusion`.
  - **Bodies**, extruded through their depth band `[z0, z1]`, as in ADR 0007. Mounted parts
    therefore sit behind their parents. Bodies are shaded near to far on the rear render's
    ramp, and clashing bodies are red, from the same `clash:` checks.
  - **Wires**, along the 3D path the length calculation uses, at their estimated outer
    diameter.
  - **Looms**, along their routes, at the bundle diameter, see-through.
  - **Bend lines**, dashed on the pilot's face.
- **Never invent a dimension.**
  - A body of unknown depth is a flat amber outline at the front of its band.
  - A bezel of unknown protrusion is drawn flat.
  - A part with a depth but no body outline is not drawn behind the panel.
  - Off-panel parts are not drawn; their wires run to where they are placed.
  - The panel is drawn flat (developed), since templates give no bend angle.
  - Each of these is listed in the view's notes, by part name.
- **No polygon booleans (ADR 0005).** A cut that overlaps another cut or crosses the outline
  cannot be triangulated as a hole without one. It is left out of the plate and noted; the
  front checks already flag it as an error.
- **Renderer: three.js** (MIT), `WebGLRenderer` with `OrbitControls`.
  - Solids are `ExtrudeGeometry` from flattened outlines (`toPolygon`, within `FLATTEN_TOL`),
    with edge lines for a drawing-like look.
  - Wires and looms are straight tube segments with a ball at each bend. Routes have sharp
    corners, so a smoothed curve would misstate them.
  - The scene renders on demand, not in a loop.
- **Loaded lazily.** `App.tsx` imports the view with `React.lazy`, so three.js is a separate
  chunk (about 153 kB gzip) fetched when the 3D tab first opens. The editor's first load grows
  by under 1 kB.
- **Commands.** `view.3d` opens the view. Inside it, `1` front, `2` rear, `3` above and
  behind, and `F` fit go through a small bridge (`view3d/camera.ts`), so the command registry
  does not import three.js.
- **Axes.** three's x and y are the panel's x and y; three's z is −depth. The pilot looks from
  +z and the fitter from −z, so the rear camera sees the same mirrored picture as the rear
  view.
- **Caption.** The view says it is built from catalogue data, is a planning aid, and is not to
  scale for cutting. Nothing in it suggests approval.

## Alternatives considered

- **Hand-written WebGL2 plus earcut.** No library, but camera, picking, lighting and
  triangulation with holes would all be ours to write and maintain. three.js does all of this,
  and in a lazy chunk it costs the editor nothing until it is used.
- **Babylon.js or a CAD kernel (OpenCascade WASM).** Much larger. A kernel would give real
  booleans and STEP export, but the view only needs extrusions.
- **Isometric SVG from the existing scene code.** Light, but hidden-surface removal between
  stacked bodies is the hard part, and orbiting would mean redrawing everything.

## Consequences

- `three` is a runtime dependency and `@types/three` a dev one, both MIT. Some packages
  `@types/three` pulls in are dev-only and never shipped; one of them,
  `@dimforge/rapier3d-compat`, is Apache-2.0.
- The 3D view is a picture of the 2.5D model, so it has the same limits. Bodies are prisms,
  and the world-axis offsets of mounted children do not rotate with their parent (ADR 0007).
- Parked in `docs/ideas.md`: glTF or STL export, a 3D still among the renders, folding the
  flange once templates carry bend angles, a depth-clip slider in 3D, and bracket parts.
